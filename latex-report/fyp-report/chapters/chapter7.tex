\chapter{Certificate Analytics and Dashboard}
\label{chap:analytics-api-dashboard}

This chapter describes how the platform transforms raw certificate data into
analytical insights through a combination of pre-computed materialized results
and live queries. It presents the six analytical families computed by the
processing framework, the security-oriented risk assessment model that ranks
individual certificates by observable risk signals, the Certificate Authority
ranking formula that compares issuer-level certificate quality, and the
interactive dashboard that exposes these capabilities through structured API
endpoints and visual pages.

\section{Analytics Processing Framework}
\label{sec:generic-precomputation-framework}

The analytical layer combines materialized result documents with live queries
over the certificate dataset. Materialized processing first creates the
database indexes required by the analytical workloads and then computes
six scope-aware analytical result families, which are stored separately 
for efficient retrieval. Live queries complement these precomputed results
by accessing the main certificate dataset directly whenever current certificate
records, detailed views, selected counts, risk candidates, or time-series
information are required. Figure~\ref{fig:analytics-materialization-flow}
illustrates the relationship between the materialized analytics pipeline and
the live query path.

The precomputation framework is a single orchestrator script that sequences
the creation of database indexes followed by the execution of six independent
analytical modules. Each module reads from the main certificate collection,
computes its aggregations, and writes the results into a dedicated results
database. Because the materialized results are recomputed on a fixed cycle
rather than updated incrementally, they represent a coherent point-in-time
snapshot of each analytical family. All analytical families support a global
view and configured country-level domain scopes. Live queries complement these
snapshots by reading directly from the main dataset for views that require
current data, such as recent issuance activity and near-expiry windows.

capabilities.

\section{Certificate and Security Analytics}
\label{sec:cert-analytics}

This section describes the certificate-level analytical families computed by
the processing framework.

\subsection{Validity and Geographic Analysis}

Validity processing converts the encoded certificate lifetime into days and
calculates average, minimum, maximum, and duration-group statistics. It also
supports issuance and expiration timelines together with live 7-, 30-, and
90-day expiry windows. Geographic categories are derived from the domain
suffix through a configured mapping. They describe domain namespace and must
not be interpreted as verified physical location.

\subsection{Signature, Hash, and Encryption Analysis}

Certificates are grouped by signature algorithm, hash family, key algorithm,
and key size. SHA-1 is treated as deprecated, while MD5 and MD2 are treated as
critical hash choices. Live encryption views count selected RSA and ECDSA
algorithm-size combinations. The resulting score and strength labels are
project-defined analytical indicators rather than standardized security
ratings.

\subsection{SAN Analysis}

The SAN family calculates total and average SAN counts, single-name and
multi-name distributions, wildcard occurrence, count buckets, and common
suffix groups. Bounded certificate references connect aggregate groups to
paginated certificate drill-down.

\subsection{Shared-Key Analysis}

Shared-key analysis identifies public keys that appear in more than one
distinct certificate. When multiple certificates share the same public-key
fingerprint, they are grouped together. Each group records affected
certificates, domains, SANs, issuers, key characteristics, and summary counts.
Exposure labels use the number of certificates and SANs to support
prioritization, but a shared key does not by itself establish private-key
compromise or unsafe cross-owner use.

\subsection{Trend Analysis}

Trend analysis tracks how certificate characteristics evolve over time.
Issuance and expiration timelines show the number of certificates created and
scheduled to expire in each period. Signature-algorithm adoption charts how
usage has shifted, for instance from SHA-1 toward SHA-256. Validation-level
trends compare the adoption of Domain Validation (DV), Organization Validation
(OV), and Extended Validation (EV) over time. Key-size series plot the
distribution of RSA modulus lengths across the dataset. Expiration views group
certificates by their \texttt{notAfter} dates and are not predictive models.

\section{Security-Oriented Risk Assessment}
\label{sec:vulnerability-risk-model}

Beyond the aggregate analytics families, the platform maintains a
certificate-level risk view that ranks individual certificates by the number
and severity of observable security signals. The view forms a bounded union of
certificates matching security-relevant signals, removes duplicate records,
applies the score in Table~\ref{tab:vulnerability-score}, and ranks the
resulting candidates.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \caption{Application-defined certificate risk score}
  \label{tab:vulnerability-score}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\RaggedRight\arraybackslash}p{0.33\textwidth}>{\Centering\arraybackslash}p{0.11\textwidth}Y@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Points} & \textbf{Condition} \\
    \midrule
    Expired certificate & $+30$ & Certificate status is expired \\
    Shared public key & $+30$ & Public-key fingerprint belongs to a shared-key group \\
    Weak encryption & $+20$ & RSA modulus length is below 2,048 bits \\
    Long validity & $+10$ & Encoded certificate lifetime exceeds 398 days \\
    Certificate-quality findings & $+1$ to $+10$ & One point per ZLint error and one point per two warnings, capped at ten \\
    Positive characteristic & $-5$ each & Current validity, strong key, shorter lifetime, and absence of lint findings \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The final value is clamped to the interval $[0,100]$ and classified as
\texttt{Critical} (85 or above), \texttt{High} (70--84),
\texttt{Medium} (40--69), or \texttt{Low} (1--39). Scores of zero are not
returned, meaning a certificate must exhibit at least one risk factor to appear
in the view. Because candidate queries are bounded and the weights are defined
by the application, the result is a prioritization aid rather than proof of
vulnerability, compromise, or exhaustive risk coverage.

\section{Certificate Authority Analytics and Ranking}
\label{sec:ca-analytics-ranking}

The Certificate Authority (CA) view aggregates the analytics above to the
issuer level. CA analysis groups leaf certificates by issuer organization,
validation level, self-signed status, and issuer-related characteristics.
Comparative ranking is based on five component groups shown in
Table~\ref{tab:ca-score-components}. Each component is computed as the mean of
several sub-metrics, ensuring that no single factor dominates the overall
score.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \caption{Certificate Authority ranking components}
  \label{tab:ca-score-components}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\RaggedRight\arraybackslash}p{0.25\textwidth}>{\RaggedRight\arraybackslash}p{0.29\textwidth}Y@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Component} & \textbf{Principal inputs} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
    \midrule
    Core hygiene & Weighted ZLint errors and warnings plus selected critical findings & Measures certificate-quality findings \\
    Cryptographic health & Key length, algorithm family, and public-key reuse & Represents cryptographic and key-management indicators \\
    Operational stability & Certificate authority and operational indicator values & Captures operational characteristics defined by the model \\
    Policy compliance & Extended key usage, policy identifiers, validation level, and name constraints & Represents policy-related certificate attributes \\
    Risk factors & Issuer-country category, authority-access information, and revocation-service presence & Adds contextual risk and revocation indicators \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

For certificate $i$, the ranking score is the mean of the five normalized
component values:

\begin{equation}
  S_i = 100 \times
  \frac{H_{\mathrm{core}} + H_{\mathrm{crypto}} + H_{\mathrm{operational}}
  + H_{\mathrm{policy}} + H_{\mathrm{risk}}}{5}.
  \label{eq:ca-score}
\end{equation}

The score for a Certificate Authority is obtained by averaging the scores of
its included leaf certificates. The output also records the number of
certificates contributing to the ranking and the component averages. The
formula supports transparent comparison within the platform but has not been
externally calibrated as an industry security rating.

\section{API and Interactive Dashboard}
\label{sec:django-api-design-filtering}

The Django backend exposes a JSON API that serves all dashboard views. Each
API endpoint maps to one or more analytical queries, supporting configurable
country scope, pagination, and certificate-level filtering by status, issuer,
text search, cryptographic attributes, validity windows, SAN characteristics,
shared-key membership, and risk signals.

The frontend consumes these endpoints through a typed API client that handles
request construction and response parsing. A global SWR provider manages data
fetching with automatic deduplication, revalidation, and caching, while React
contexts coordinate scope, search, theme, and dashboard state across pages.
Figure~\ref{fig:frontend-component-flow} summarises the presentation
architecture.

Users navigate the dashboard through a sidebar that lists each analytical
family as a dedicated page. The landing page presents a platform overview with
total, active, expiring-soon, and expired certificate counts alongside a
searchable certificate table. A scope switcher in the header allows users to
filter all views by country-level domain scope without leaving the current
page.

The dashboard is organised into three broad categories. Health and status
views provide a quick pulse on the dataset---the overview page for summary
counts, and the active-versus-expired page for a deeper breakdown by
certificate state. Certificate analytics pages cover validity periods,
Subject Alternative Name distributions, signature and hash algorithm
analysis, geographic breakdown by issuer country, and the relationship
between Certificate Authorities and the domains they cover. Security-oriented
views highlight shared-key reuse groups, the ranked risk assessment for
individual certificates, and the comparative CA ranking based on certificate
quality metrics.

Every analytical page displays summary cards, distribution charts, and
pagination tables designed for exploration. Clicking a chart segment or a
table row navigates to a filtered certificate list or to the full certificate
detail page, which displays the complete set of parsed fields including
issuer, subject, validity, fingerprints, SANs, key usage, and ZLint results.
This linking creates a consistent path from high-level trends down to
individual certificate evidence.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter presented the analytical framework that converts the collected
certificate dataset into actionable insights. The precomputation engine
materializes six analytical families on a fixed cycle, while live queries
handle views that require current data. The risk assessment model provides a
transparent, rule-based scoring system for certificate triage, and the CA
ranking formula enables comparative evaluation of issuer-level certificate
quality. The interactive dashboard ties these capabilities together through a
unified interface with scope-aware filtering and drill-down navigation. The
following chapter evaluates the overall platform against its design objectives
and discusses the quality of the collected dataset.
