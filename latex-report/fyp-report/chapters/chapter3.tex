\section{Stakeholders and Use Cases}
\label{sec:stakeholders-use-cases}

SSL Guardian is intended for four primary stakeholder groups:
\textbf{cybersecurity researchers}, \textbf{security analysts},
\textbf{system administrators}, and \textbf{students and educators}. These
users are interested in understanding SSL/TLS certificate ecosystems,
investigating certificate characteristics, identifying security-relevant
patterns, and conducting large-scale certificate analysis. The platform supports 
these objectives by providing an interactive environment for exploring 
certificate information, analyzing security-relevant characteristics, and 
performing comparative SSL/TLS certificate analysis

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.08}
  \caption{Primary Stakeholders and Their Objectives}
  \label{tab:primary-stakeholders}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\RaggedRight\arraybackslash}p{0.28\textwidth}Y@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Stakeholder} & \textbf{Primary Objective} \\
    \midrule
    Cybersecurity researchers & Study SSL/TLS certificate ecosystems, deployment trends, and security characteristics. \\
    Security analysts & Investigate expiration, weak cryptographic configurations, public-key reuse, and other security indicators. \\
    System administrators & Examine certificate deployments and lifecycle information to support operational awareness. \\
    Students and educators & Explore SSL/TLS certificates and Public Key Infrastructure concepts for learning and research. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The primary use cases involve searching, filtering, and inspecting certificate
records; examining validity, issuer, cryptographic, Subject Alternative Name,
and shared-key characteristics; identifying security-related indicators; and
comparing Certificate Authorities using certificate-derived measures. Users
may also explore trends and visual summaries across global and configured
country-level scopes. Together, these activities support research,
investigation, operational awareness, and education, and establish the
user-oriented expectations formalized in the following functional
requirements.

\section{Functional Requirements}
\label{sec:derived-functional-requirements}

The functional requirements define the principal services and observable 
capabilities that the developed platform must provide to satisfy stakeholder 
needs. They describe the major functions expected from the system while 
remaining independent of the underlying implementation.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.08}
  \caption{Functional Requirements of the Proposed System}
  \label{tab:derived-functional-requirements}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\Centering\arraybackslash}p{0.10\textwidth}Y@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{ID} & \textbf{Functional Requirement} \\
    \midrule
    FR-01 & The system shall accept supported domain datasets and perform automated SSL/TLS certificate acquisition for target domains. \\
    FR-02 & The system shall retrieve SSL/TLS certificates presented by target domains and transform them into structured X.509 certificate records. \\
    FR-03 & The system shall retain structured certificate information together with the metadata required for analysis, search, and visualization. \\
    FR-04 & The system shall process Certificate Transparency observations to distinguish newly observed domains from certificate renewal candidates. \\
    FR-05 & The system shall maintain the certificate dataset by incorporating newly acquired and renewed certificates and refreshing analytical results after each update cycle. \\
    FR-06 & The system shall generate analytical results for major certificate characteristics, Certificate Authorities, and supported global and country-level scopes. \\
    FR-07 & The system shall identify and present expired certificates, weak cryptographic configurations, public-key reuse, and certificate-quality findings as analytical indicators for further investigation. \\
    FR-08 & The system shall provide certificate search, filtering, detailed inspection, analytical summaries, and trend visualization capabilities. \\
    FR-09 & The system shall present certificate information and analytical results through an interactive web-based dashboard. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

services, and interactive visualization. Each technology was selected
according to its suitability for a specific role, compatibility with the
surrounding components, and support for maintainable development.

\section{System Components and Context}
\label{sec:system-context}

The platform is organized into five cooperating logical components that connect
Internet certificate sources with user-facing analytical results. Their
principal responsibilities are summarized below.

\noindent\textbf{Certificate Acquisition.}
Obtains and structures SSL/TLS certificates from supported domain inputs and
Internet TLS endpoints.

\noindent\textbf{CT-Assisted Dataset Maintenance.}
Uses public Certificate Transparency observations to identify newly observed
and renewed domain candidates and maintain dataset freshness.

\noindent\textbf{Analytics Processing.}
Produces global and country-level summaries, trends, Certificate Authority
comparisons, and security-relevant indicators.

\noindent\textbf{Application Services.}
Makes certificate records and analytical results available for search,
filtering, detailed inspection, and presentation.

\noindent\textbf{Interactive Dashboard.}
Enables stakeholders to explore certificate records, summaries, comparisons,
tables, and visualizations.

Internet TLS endpoints supply certificates for domain-based acquisition, while
Certificate Transparency provides observations for dataset maintenance. The
collected information supports analytics and application services, and users
access the results through the dashboard. The experimental IP-based certificate
crawler operates independently and does not contribute to the primary
domain-based dashboard dataset. The detailed architecture, data flow, component
interactions, and data design are presented in
Chapter~\ref{chap:architecture-data-design}.
