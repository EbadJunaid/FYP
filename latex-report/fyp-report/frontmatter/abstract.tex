\TemplateFrontHeading{ABSTRACT}

SSL/TLS certificates secure traffic across millions of websites, yet many use
weak algorithms, carry expired validity, or reuse public keys across unrelated
domains. Certificate Transparency logs make these certificates publicly
observable, but looking at certificates one at a time reveals only isolated
details, not the broader patterns needed to assess issuer behaviour,
cryptographic practices, or certificate quality. This report presents SSL
Guardian, an integrated platform that collects, maintains, analyzes, and
presents large-scale certificate data through a single workflow.

The platform acquires certificates from Internet TLS endpoints using a
concurrent, domain-based crawler with persistent task state and stale-task
recovery, supported by a separate experimental IP-based crawler. A
CT-assisted maintenance pipeline observes public logs, separates renewal
candidates from previously unseen domains, re-crawls both, and merges the
results so that the dataset stays current. Parsed X.509 records are stored in
MongoDB and processed into six materialized analytical families---validity,
geography, signature and key, Subject Alternative Names, shared keys, and
Certificate Authority---complemented by live queries for time-sensitive views.
A Django service exposes these results, and a Next.js dashboard provides
scope-aware search, filtering, visualization, and certificate-level drill-down
across global and country-level scopes.

The platform was evaluated through structural inspection, static analysis,
interface traceability, and one IP-crawler experiment comparing acquisition
with and without Server Name Indication. The analytical scores, Certificate
Authority rankings, and shared-key labels are project-defined indicators for
further investigation rather than externally calibrated security ratings or
proof of compromise, and integration, concurrency, and performance testing
remain future work.
